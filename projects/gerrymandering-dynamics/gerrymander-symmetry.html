Seats–votes curve · the symmetry test

Blue's actual response Its mirror (a fair map)
Reading it: A symmetric map's solid curve sits on top of its dashed mirror — swap the parties' vote shares and the seats swap too. A gerrymander pulls the solid curve away from the mirror: at a 50/50 vote (the centre line) one side already holds more seats, because cracking & packing make that side's votes cheaper per seat. Bias = the vertical gap at the centre line. Disproportional ≠ unfair; asymmetric is unfair.
