Reading bias vs structure: with every multiplier at ×1 the curves still fan apart — that's the neutral structural effect (who each party runs against), not rigging. Push a party's bias above ×1 and its solid curve lifts clear of its dashed ghost: the lines now convert that party's votes into seats better than an identically-placed rival's, which is exactly what a gerrymander does. The Gallagher number blends both sources; the gap between a curve and its ghost isolates the bias alone. Disproportionality (distance from the diagonal) and responsiveness (shared ρ) are properties of the whole system; bias is the part that singles out a party.
