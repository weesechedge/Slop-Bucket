Gerrymandering Dynamics

Four interactive explainers on how district lines and electoral systems turn votes into seats — moving from concrete map-drawing to the abstract seats–votes curve. The throughline: disproportionality (seats drifting from votes) is not the same as unfairness — a map is unfair when its vote→seat response is asymmetric, paying one party more seats than its rival for the very same vote share.

1
The Symmetry Test
Twenty-five fixed voters (15 blue, 10 orange); only the district lines move. Watch cracking and packing bend the response off its mirror, with efficiency gap and partisan bias.
