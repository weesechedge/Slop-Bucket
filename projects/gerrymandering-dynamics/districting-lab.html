How to read the curve: the x-axis sweeps the Focus party's vote share (rival absorbing the difference, the other three parties held fixed). The solid line is the Focus party's seats under the current map; the dashed line is what a symmetric map would give it — the seats the rival earns at that same share. When the two lines sit on top of each other, the lines are fair: identical vote shares pay out identically whichever party holds them. When the solid line floats above the dashed one, the map is rigged toward Focus; below, against it. The marker is wherever your sliders currently put Focus. Note this is separate from disproportionality: a map can be near-symmetric yet wildly disproportional, because single-member districts never promised proportionality — only, at best, even-handedness.
