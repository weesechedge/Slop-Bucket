A Day in the APSthe full day job of a public servant, simulated at one second per second

You are an EL1 in the Assurance & Coordination Section of a Commonwealth department. The day is 7 hours 36 minutes — the APS standard day — plus the half hour of lunch you will not take. It runs from 09:00 to 17:06 at one second per second. There is no fast-forward, because there isn't one at work either. Email arrives, meetings land on top of meetings, Teams fails in the specific ways Teams fails, the assurance template is returned, and the ministerial goes up the clearance chain and comes back down it. The day runs whether or not this tab is open: close it at 10 and come back at 3, and you will find 3 o'clock's inbox waiting, exactly as it would be.

Two ways to play. Work it — you do the clicking, and everything you do costs real minutes. Just watch — hands off, and you watch someone else's screen for eight hours, which was the original brief.

Bring your own assurance

the actual point of this

The Assurance Portal tab ships with an eighteen-question gate submission of the usual shape: quantify the non-financial benefit in dollars, attach the endorsement of the thing that is not yet endorsed, provide a minimum of twelve risks and a maximum of ten. At the bottom of that tab there is a box. Paste your real assurance questions into it — one per line — and they become the template. Then complete them here, in real time, while the inbox fills and the meetings start and the portal times out. That is a better assurance testing device than a meeting about the assurance testing device. Nothing you paste leaves your browser; it is stored in this browser's local storage and nowhere else.
